## 1. THE COLOUR ANSWER — measured from the designer's own work

I sampled the actual pixels of the brand board, the brand patterns, and the
social-media designs the brand designer (Hajer Majdoub, 2023) delivered. These
are her own compositions — the most authoritative statement of how this brand is
supposed to look, more authoritative than a hex list on a PDF page.

### 1.1 What she actually uses

| Asset | Dominant colour | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Facebook banner | **`#EAE6DE`** warm ivory | **94.8 %** |
| Facebook banner (alt) | **`#BE6D4A`** terracotta | **69.2 %** |
| Profile picture | **`#842906`** burnt sienna | **89.6 %** |
| Profile picture (alt) | `#BE6D4A` terracotta | 88.1 % |
| Brand board | white + **`#A16E5C`** rosewood + `#EDE6DE` | 35 % / 27 % / 7 % |
| Brand patterns | white ground + `#96441A` motifs | 68 % / 5 % |

Supporting tones that recur: `#E3BC96` sand, `#C9A392` clay, `#D6C0B2` rose-clay,
`#7A4231` cocoa.

### 1.2 The two findings that matter

**FINDING 1 — her ground is NOT white. It is `#EAE6DE`, a warm ivory.**
94.8 % of a banner is that single colour. My light skin drove the site to **79 %
pure `#FFFFFF`**, which is why it came back rejected: technically light, but cold
and clinical. It stripped out exactly the warmth that makes this brand feel like
a celebrations brand. *Light was the right direction; **white** was the wrong
destination.*

**FINDING 2 — her brown is not `#96421A`, and never near-black.**
The palette we were told to use has Warm Mocha `#96421A` (harsh, saturated
orange-brown) and we paired it with espresso `#2E1A0D` (almost black). She uses
neither at scale. Her working browns are **`#BE6D4A`** (soft terracotta) and
**`#842906`** (burnt sienna) — both warmer and softer. **There is no near-black
anywhere in her work.** The espresso slabs were never in the brand.

So the client's *"le site est trop sombre… je veux pas le marron"* and
*"the light version is not ok"* are both consistent, and both correct. He was
never objecting to brown. He was objecting to **the wrong browns** — first as
dark slabs, then removed entirely and replaced by cold white.

### 1.3 The proposed system — every value measured from his files

| Token | Hex | Role | Contrast |
|---|---|---|---|
| `--noor-ground` | **`#EAE6DE`** | page background — warm ivory, never white | — |
| `--noor-surface` | `#FFFFFF` | cards float *on* the ground | 1.24:1 vs ground (visible separation) |
| `--noor-ink` | **`#7A4231`** | body text and headings — warm cocoa, not black | **6.4:1** on ground ✅ AA |
| `--noor-action` | **`#842906`** | buttons, links, prices | **7.3:1** on ground ✅ AA · white text on it **9.1:1** ✅ AAA |
| `--noor-terracotta` | **`#BE6D4A`** | brand fills, bands, hovers | 3.1:1 — **large elements only, never body text** |
| `--noor-sand` | `#E3BC96` | soft highlight, chips | decorative |
| `--noor-clay` | `#C9A392` | borders, dividers, muted text on white | decorative / large |

**The rule that fixes both failures:** the page is **warm ivory**, not white and
not brown. White is reserved for cards, so products lift off the background.
Brown appears as **`#842906` type and buttons** and **`#BE6D4A` accents** — never
as a full-width slab, and never as near-black.

`#842906` on white is 9.1:1, so buttons finally pass AAA — the old `#96421A`
managed 6.8:1 and the WooCommerce olive was failing outright at 3.4:1.

### 3.1 The accessories — and the problem they create

From `ACCESSOIRES pour Arches_.docx`:

| Parent arch | Accessories offered | Prices |
|---|---|---|
| Arche carrée 2 m | 2 Rideaux blancs · Lot de 4 fleurs | 20 € · 15 € · **les 2 = 25 €** |
| Arche ronde dorée 2 m | 2 Drapés 6 m · Lot de 4 fleurs | 20 € · 15 € · **les 2 = 25 €** |
| Arche ronde blanche 1,80 m | 2 Drapés 6 m · Lot de 4 fleurs | 20 € · 15 € · **les 2 = 25 €** |
| Arches en U lot de 3 | 2 Drapés 6 m · Lot de 4 fleurs | 20 € · 15 € · **les 2 = 25 €** |
| Support panneau | Lot de 4 fleurs | 15 € |

Two structural facts fall out of this:

**(a) The accessory list differs per arch.** The square arch takes *rideaux*
(curtains); the round ones take *drapés*. The support takes only flowers.
✅ Already supported — options are curated per-product via Linked Products → Upsells.

**(b) 🔴 THERE IS A COMBO PRICE, AND THE CODE DOES NOT SUPPORT IT.**
20 € + 15 € = 35 €, but both together = **25 €**. That is a **10 € discount**
that only exists when both boxes are ticked. `inc/rental-options.php` as shipped
prices each option independently — it would charge 35 €, **overcharging every
customer who takes both**, which is the combination he is actively promoting.

This is the single most important gap found in the whole folder. Fix in §5.2.

**Note also:** this answers the question he never answered. The prices are
**flat per rental**, not per day. The `_noor_option_pricing` default (`flat`) is
correct, and the question can be dropped from the message.

## 5. THE PLAN

### 5.1 Re-skin to the measured palette (replaces the failed light skin)

Rework `assets/css/noor-light.css` into `noor-warm.css` with the §1.3 tokens:
ground `#EAE6DE`, cards white, text `#7A4231`, actions `#842906`, accents
`#BE6D4A`/`#E3BC96`. Keep every structural fix already made and verified —
the split hero, the white marquee, the killed espresso slabs, the Elementor
`!important` overrides, the `--noor-tint` hover fix, the mocha footer logo.
**Only the colour values change.** Then re-measure per §9.1 and expect roughly
70–80 % warm-ivory ground, ~0 % near-black, brown present but never as a slab.

### 5.2 🔴 Combo pricing for options — required before any option goes live

`inc/rental-options.php` needs a combo rule: *when options X and Y are both
selected on the same parent booking, the pair costs Z instead of X+Y.*

Design that fits the existing module: a `_noor_option_combo` meta on the
**parent** rental holding `{option_sku_a, option_sku_b, combo_price}`, applied in
the same `woocommerce_before_calculate_totals` pass that already sets absolute
prices — so it stays idempotent and no `did_action` guard is needed. The
cart line must say *"Forfait 2 options : 25 € au lieu de 35 €"*, or customers
will not notice they saved anything.

### 5.3 Extend the SVPI importer — it cannot currently carry this data

The plugin reads: `name, sku, type, short_description, description_html,
regular_price, sale_price, stock_quantity, categories, tags, brand,
gallery_images, attributes, variations`.

**It does not read `meta` or `upsells`.** Both are needed:

| Needed | For |
|---|---|
| `_noor_block_price`, `_noor_block_days` | the 25 €/4-jours forfait |
| `_noor_deposit_amount` | the 50 € deposit |
| `_noor_option_pricing` | flat vs per-day |
| `_noor_option_combo` | the 25 € combo (§5.2) |
| **upsell ids/skus** | **which accessories appear under which arch — the whole options feature** |

Add two keys to the item schema — `meta: {}` and `upsell_skus: []` — resolved
after all products exist (SKU → id in a second pass, since an arch references
accessories that may import later). Small change, and it makes the whole import
reproducible instead of hand-wired.
